Analogue signals are used to represent, transmit, and process continuous physical data—such as sound, light, temperature, pressure, and position—by varying voltage, current, or frequency. They are essential in real-time applications, including audio recording/reproduction, radio/television broadcasting, sensor monitoring, and traditional telephony. 

Key Applications of Analogue Signals
Audio & Sound Systems: Microphones and speakers use analog signals to convert sound waves into electrical signals and back again.
Sensor & Instrumentation: Devices like thermometers, pressure gauges, and light sensors produce continuous voltage or current (e.g., 4-20mA) to monitor environmental changes.
Communication Systems: Radio (AM/FM) and traditional TV broadcasting use continuous waveforms to transmit information.
Control Systems: Actuators and motor controllers use analog signals to adjust, for example, the position of a valve or the speed of a motor.
Data Conversion: While many systems are now digital, analogue signals are still used in the initial stage of data acquisition, which are then converted into digital data for computer processing. 

Advantages of Using Analogue Signals
Real-time representation: They accurately mimic natural, continuously changing physical phenomena.
High Density: They can represent an infinite range of values within a given range.
Simplicity: Often easier to process for direct, simple applications.
